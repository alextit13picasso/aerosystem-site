From 12-Second Friction to Instant Checkout: B2B App Widget Refactoring

In early 2021, a well-known enterprise operating in marketing communications and servicing urban transit networks approached us. Their goal was to increase reliability and deployment speed across two libraries that had been acquired as separate, independent startups. Our task was to perform a comprehensive audit, full code review, and isolate metrics that required structural optimization.

Following our established processes, we initiated the project with deep discovery sessions with the business stakeholders. During these discussions, we noticed an unaddressed product anomaly: management frequently referenced a "long and unstable ticket purchase flow via transport numbers and QR codes." Initially, this friction was treated as low priority, and we proceeded directly into the core technical code audit.

While analyzing the product analytics data, we discovered a critical bottleneck: the core transit payment flow on Category 3 devices (mid-range hardware with 2+ CPU cores) took over 12 seconds to complete. This user flow latency persisted even on high-speed internet connections. From a product engineering standpoint, this was highly inefficient, especially considering this single process generated approximately 12% of the company's total revenue. The flow suffered from major flaws: it was excessively long, failed unexpectedly during critical steps, and forced users through intrusive actions like PIN inputs or SMS verification codes.

Although this module was initially outside our direct scope of work, the potential loss of revenue for our client made it impossible to ignore. We aggregated the telemetry metrics and initiated a targeted technical discussion with the business stakeholders. It turned out they were aware of the friction but had defused its priority due to a backlogged pipeline of other tasks. We proposed a strategy to restructure our ongoing refactoring schedule to isolate and fix this checkout funnel within the existing budget limits.

Once authorized, we engineered a comprehensive three-part system optimization architecture:

1. Upgrading the authentication subsystem of our module to issue decoupled, long-lived tokens;

2. Modifying the core host application's auth subsystem to process these requests correctly;

3. Engineering a lightweight native UI/UX entry point directly on the user's home screen.

First, we designed a token generation mechanism that bypasses standard short-lived JWTs. These decoupled tokens possess a lifespan of approximately 3 months before requiring reuse rotation. This change allowed the system to securely reuse tokens for authenticated API calls to protected endpoints outside the host application's active lifecycle, expanding the authorized zone while preserving its security integrity.

Next, we had to ensure the host application could intercept and parse responses from these background requests smoothly at the user flow level. However, the existing application architecture contained over 120 separate entry points into the checkout flow. Modifying this system directly introduced unacceptable regression risks. To mitigate this, we mapped every single ingress route into the flow and abstracted them into a rigid, unified integration contract.

We enforced this architectural boundary by writing a custom validation rule on our CI/CD pipeline. Every single Merge Request was evaluated against this contract; if an ingress rule was broken, the pipeline failed automatically and blocked the merge. This automated enforcement stabilized the integration point between our refactored module and the legacy architecture, backed by 100% unit test coverage for the transition logic.

With the backend integration boundary secured, we turned our focus to the UX layer. To bypass the heavy host application launch sequence, we targeted the Android App Widget framework. We designed clean, native widgets—initially built using classical XML layouts and later migrated to Jetpack Glance. Leveraging the decoupled token architecture, the widget dynamically rendered minimal, context-specific checkout data right on the home screen. A single tap on the widget routed the user directly to the final execution point of the payment flow.

In coordination with the client's marketing department, we executed a phased, tiered deployment strategy. We gradually rolled out a specialized one-click onboarding flow to 1%, 5%, 10%, 50%, and finally 100% of the active user base. This onboarding sequence allowed transit passengers to pin the widget to their home screen instantly. By removing PIN inputs, SMS verifications, and multiple screen transitions, users could complete their ticket purchases almost instantaneously.

Business Impact & Outcomes

By eliminating legacy overhead, we completely removed two full UI screens, four separate Data Sources, and dozens of redundant architectural entities. Abstracting the integration contract sharply localized our feature's scope of impact, which inherently optimized our development and testing timeline. By structurally removing dead code, we eliminated the future technical debt required to maintain it.

The post-deployment ecosystem featured two distinct ingress routes: the legacy flow (optimized and shifted closer to the main application screen) and our native home screen widget. Initial product telemetry showed a brief 1.5% drop in transaction volume during the first month, despite active onboarding prompts. However, by month two—following the 100% onboarding saturation—the funnel stabilized, delivering a net positive transaction surge of 7.3%.

Six months post-launch, the steady-state performance metrics settled at the following values:

• Ingress Traffic: User entry into the ticket purchase flow increased by +27% compared to the baseline legacy architecture.

• Checkout Conversion: The conversion rate from flow initialization to successful transaction reached a stable 87%.

• Active Volume: The native App Widget sustained a steady Monthly Active User (MAU) volume of 48,000 users, scaling the clean monthly net profit of this single feature branch by approximately $3,016 USD.
